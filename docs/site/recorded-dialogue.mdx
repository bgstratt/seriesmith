Use this when you have real recorded lines (actors, you, or TTS) instead of letting H3 clone or invent voices.

## Steps

```
cd <your projects folder>
pip install faster-whisper numpy                              # once
pip install nvidia-cublas-cu12                                # an NVIDIA card: Whisper on the GPU
# and the Whisper model: the Features window, Recorded dialogue (medium.en)

seriesmith align    Shows\ep05 audio\ep05_dialogue.wav --dry-run   # look first
seriesmith align    Shows\ep05 audio\ep05_dialogue.wav
seriesmith build    Shows\ep05
seriesmith render   Shows\ep05 --proxy
seriesmith assemble Shows\ep05 --proxy --audio master  # picture + your recording, to check sync
```

Put the recording inside the episode folder (for example `ep05\audio\`) so the series config can point at it with a relative path. faster-whisper isn't one of seriesmith's dependencies: install it and numpy (and, with an NVIDIA card, `nvidia-cublas-cu12`) in the Python align runs from: seriesmith's own server and the command line use seriesmith's Python, the editor inside ComfyUI uses ComfyUI's ([FULL_SETUP.md](/full-setup), "Sound: voices and recorded dialogue"). Its model is downloaded in the Features window (**Recorded dialogue** has `medium.en`, the default; each other model is a line of its own, **Whisper large-v3 (align)** and so on) into the Hugging Face cache; align reads it from there and never downloads it itself, so with the model not there it stops and says which line to download. The editor does the same from its dialogue track: attach the recording, then align (`seriesmith/track.py`).

## Lines from a voice model

You don't need to record anything if a voice model on a provider speaks the lines. Choose one, such as
ElevenLabs, under **Voices with:** on the Refs tab, and pick a speaker on each character's voice row. Then, in
the **Recording** window, open **Or make the lines with a voice model**:

1. **Look at the total.** Every line and character is counted before anything is sent, and a character with
   no speaker stops it.
2. **Generate them.** That's paid, one call per line. Each line is its own take, so you can redo one alone and
   choose between its takes.
3. **Lay them into the recording.** That's free. It joins the takes in script order, leaving each silent shot's
   `dur:` as a pause, and attaches the result. The model's own timings go beside it, so no Whisper is needed.
4. **Align**, as below.

If you change a line or its speaker, its take is marked as changed. Redoing a line can change its length, so
lay and align again afterwards: the shots after it move, though only the redone shot goes stale.

### A file for each character

If you have all of one character's lines in one file, from an actor or generated on a voice model's site, use
**Use a file…** on that character's row. The file should hold one voice, nobody else, with the lines in script
order. Whisper hears it and cuts it into a take per line, so it costs nothing. A line it can't hear gets no take
and is listed on the character's row. Then lay and align as above, mixing files and generated lines as you like,
and redo any single line.

Everything comes together in one recording, because a video model lip-syncs a shot to one sound. Two people
talking over each other isn't handled here: it's one line at a time.

## Recording

- Record the lines in script order, dry: no music under them. If there is music under them (a song), see **A recording with music: the vocal stem** below.
- Leave real pauses where the script has silent shots. align stretches or squeezes those shots to fit the pause, and warns you when the pause is far off.
- One file for the whole episode.

## What align changes

- **Script:** adds `audio: in-out` to every shot and turns old `dur:` lines into comments. The old copy is kept in `<episode>\_history\`.
- **Series config:** sets `audio.mode` to `source_track` and `audio.track` to your recording. The old copy is kept in `<episode>\_history\` too (the same history the editor writes, so both show the same versions).
- **Report:** writes `align_report.md` with every window and line, plus any problems it found.
- **Transcript cache:** saves the transcript as `<recording>.words.json`, so re-running is instant. Use `--retranscribe` after you replace the recording.
- **Cut points:** cuts land at the quietest point of each pause. Speaking shots are lengthened to the frame grid of the target they render on (H3's 17k+5, LTX's 8k+1) when the pause has room.

## Audio policy and retention

A speaking shot defaults to `dub_keep_foley`: H3 lip-syncs to your line, and the saver keeps H3's sound effects with the voice removed, as `_foley.wav`.

| Retention | What H3 does with your line | Default for |
|---|---|---|
| `fully_copy` | Your clip becomes the shot's entire soundtrack. Lips follow it, and no sound effects are added. | `dub` |
| `partially_copy` | Copies your line exactly and adds ambience and action sounds around it | `dub_keep_foley` |
| `reference` | Only borrows the voice and timing, as before. Lip sync is loose. | |

To override, set it per shot (`retention: fully_copy`), for the episode (`"audio": {"retention": ...}`), or for the default policy (`"audio": {"default_policy": "dub"}`). You can also pass `--policy` or `--retention` to align. Shots in `clone` and `generate` mode are unchanged.

Frame lip-sync shots as close or medium shots. The reference image's framing matters more than the prompt.

On other models a dub shot works too, without retention: on LTX (`ltx2`, `ltx2_ingredients`, `ltx2_msr`) the shot's window is held as its whole soundtrack and the picture is made to it, as `fully_copy` does on H3, so `dub_keep_foley` gets a near-silent foley bed; Seedance is sent the window as an audio reference and told to sync the lips to it.

## align options

`python -m seriesmith.align --help` lists them all. The ones you reach for:

| Flag | What it does |
|---|---|
| `--dry-run` | Report only. Changes no files. Always worth running first |
| `--retranscribe` | Ignore the cached `<recording>.words.json` after replacing the recording |
| `--words <file>` | Use word timings you already have instead of transcribing |
| `--model <name>` | Whisper model: `tiny.en`, `base.en`, `small.en`, `medium.en` (default), `large-v3`, `large-v3-turbo` (close to large-v3, several times faster) |
| `--device cuda\|cpu` | Where Whisper runs. `auto` by default |
| `--script <file>` | Pick the script when the folder holds more than one `.md` |
| `--no-snap` | Leave windows exactly as measured instead of lengthening them to the target's frame grid |
| `--vocals` | Transcribe the recording's vocal stem, and cut at the pauses in it, instead of the whole mix. Makes the stem first if it isn't there. Off by default |
| `--policy`, `--retention` | Override the defaults in the table above for the whole episode |

## A recording with music: the vocal stem

A song, or lines with music under them, can be split into its **vocal stem** (the voice alone) and the rest. seriesmith does it on your local ComfyUI with its own Vocal Stem node (torchaudio's Hybrid Demucs, which ComfyUI already has; its weights you download in the Features window, **Vocal stem**). The stem lands beside the recording as `<recording>.vocals.wav` and is kept until the recording changes. Nothing uses it unless you ask, in two places:

- **Align from the stem.** `seriesmith align <ep> --vocals`, or **from the vocal stem** in the editor's Recording window. Whisper hears the voice without the band, and the cuts land in the pauses of the singing. The series config still points at the recording.
- **Dub shots hear the stem.** `"audio": {"dub_from": "vocals"}` in the series config, or **dub shots hear the vocal stem** in the Recording window. Each dub shot's window is cut from the stem instead of the recording. The episode's own sound (the mix, `assemble --audio master`) is still the recording.

Both are off by default: on the pop song they were tried on, H3 Ref2VA lip-synced as well to the whole song as to the stem. Try the stem when a model mouths the band, or when align misses lines under loud music. The ComfyUI must have loaded seriesmith's node pack since the Vocal Stem node was added: restart it once after updating seriesmith. **Make the vocal stem** in the Recording window makes it on its own. Making a music video from a song is in [MUSIC_VIDEO.md](/music-video).

## Conform

- **Trimming:** assemble trims each timed shot back to its exact window, so the cut lines up with the recording from the first window's start. `--no-trim` turns this off.
- **Sync check:** `--audio master` lays the recording under the cut. It's a check, not a mix.
- **Final mix:** do it in Resolve. Put the cut on one track and the recording on another, starting at the time shown in `align_report.md`. Add each shot's `_foley.wav` at its start time (listed in `_shots.txt`), then music.
