A song that characters sing on screen is cut **song-first**: the song is made before the picture,
and the picture follows it. So a number isn't a block of the act's script. It's an **episode of
its own** beside its act, cut as a music video, and the act cuts into it and out again.

The example is *Ring Bright*, a spoken Christmas special with seven numbers. Its blacksmith's
song, "Hammer, Strike and Ring", is `ep02_song5`, beside the act `ep02`.

```
one_horse_open_sleigh/
  songbook/         a held picture per song, a Song cue over each
  ep02/             the act
  ep02_song5/       the number: its own series config, audio/song.wav, its script
```

## 1. The song first: a songbook

Make the song before there's any picture for it, in a scratch episode of its own.

<Steps>
  <Step title="A held picture as long as the song">
    A new episode, `songbook`, with one shot per song on the Hold model: a title card, as long as
    the song should be.

    ```
    ## sh050
    target: hold
    size: ws
    dur: 170
    A title card: Song 5, Hammer, Strike and Ring.
    ```

    It renders in a moment, on this machine, so the episode has a cut of that length.
  </Step>
  <Step title="A Song cue over it">
    Right-click the shot → **Sound → New music cue**. In the cue window's inspector set **Kind**
    to **Song**, or **Start from a preset** (pop ballad, upbeat pop) and adjust.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Its style, tempo and model">
    **Style** in words (*a rousing folk work song, anvil hits on the beat, fiddle and
    concertina, a strong baritone lead*), **Tempo**, **Meter** and **Key**. **Model**: YuE2 (the
    default, non-commercial weights), ACE-Step or MiniMax Music 3 (both fine for a commercial
    release; MiniMax's words come through clearest).
  </Step>
  <Step title="The lyrics on its sections">
    Click each section (verse, chorus, bridge) and type its **Lyrics**. A section with none isn't
    sung. A song with no lyrics at all won't render.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Render and pick">
    **Render** a few takes, listen to each whole, and **Pick** one. Each take is a wav in
    `songbook\sound\<cue>\` (song 5's are in `sound\m05\`).
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  Keep the song's whole take. The number will start about two bars before the first sung line,
  and the long band intro you cut can play under the act's last lines into the song.
</Tip>

## 2. The number as a music video

<Steps>
  <Step title="A new episode beside the act">
    `ep02_song5`, with its own `series.json`: a copy of the act's, with the song as its
    recording.

    ```json
    "audio": {"mode": "source_track", "track": "audio/song.wav",
              "default_policy": "dub", "retention": "fully_copy"}
    ```

    The act's own config stays on `generate`. Copy the picked take in as `audio\song.wav`, cut to
    start a bar or two before the singing.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Every sung line as dialogue">
    The script has every sung line in order, word for word. The lines that matter on camera are
    sung there; the rest are `(V.O.)` over the action:

    ```
    ## sh040
    who: fanny
    size: mcu
    plate: smithy_allnight_hearth_close
    At the blazing hearth, Fanny tosses a dented kettle into the glowing crucible and grins, singing, her eyes off to frame right.
    FANNY (singing): Melt the kettle, melt the nail,
    sound: a kettle clanking into iron, the coals hissing

    ## sh050
    size: ecu
    plate: smithy_allnight_hearth_close
    An extreme close-up of a rusty door hinge and a battered tin pail tumbling into a glowing iron crucible and slumping into the white-hot coals.
    FANNY (V.O., singing): Melt the hinge and the rusty pail,
    sound: scrap clattering into iron, metal sizzling
    ```

    Keep a singing shot to about 6 seconds. Shots with no lines (the band, the forge, a
    cutaway) fill the pauses.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Align">
    **⋯ → Recording** → **Dry run**, then **Align**. Align hears the song, finds each line, and
    gives every shot an `audio:` window, end to end through the song. Check `align_report.md`'s
    coverage: a line sung over a loud band may need **from the vocal stem**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Build, then render the proxies">
    **Build** (render reads the built shotlist, so always build after editing the script), then
    render. Singing shots dub to their window, with `retention: fully_copy`, so the lips follow
    the song.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Cut on the beat">
    Trims in the timeline snap to the song's beats (hold **Alt** to trim freely).
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
  **A singing shot with a long silent stretch at its end can drift out of sync**, even with
  `fully_copy`: the model stretches the singing over the silence. Split the window instead: the
  sung part as one shot, the rest as a second shot (`continuous: latent` keeps them one
  movement). *Ring Bright*'s reprise needed this.
</Warning>

## 3. The joins

The song should start and end mid-movement, as if the act carried straight on into it.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Into the song">
    In the act, on the last shot before the song: right-click its take → **Frames → Save this
    frame as a picture** on its last frame. In the number, drop that PNG on the first shot's
    first keyframe (or save it as `refs\shots\sh010\first.png` and give the shot `first: import`).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Out of it">
    The same the other way: the number's last frame as the first keyframe of the act's first
    shot after the song.
  </Step>
</Steps>

H3 Ref2VA, Singularity and Hybrid open a shot on its first keyframe, so the character walks into
the song and out of it in one movement.

## 4. Export and join

Export the number with the song unbroken: **its song as it came**, `seriesmith assemble ep02_song5
--audio master`, or the song on the Music lane at 0 dB with the Dialogue track muted (the export
then brings it to the loudness target). Then join the acts and numbers in order in your editor:
each one's `assemble --xml` lays it out on tracks, with its stems.

## Singing in the characters' own voices

The song models can't sing in a given voice: the take's singer is the model's. *Ring Bright*
changed each take's singing into its characters' voices **outside seriesmith**, with
SoulX-Singer (open source, zero-shot from a short voice sample):

1. The take's vocal stem, cut at each sung line's window.
2. Each piece converted to its singer's voice sample. Keep the pitch shift at 0 (or ±12): an
   automatic shift can land off key.
3. A chorus sung by a group can't become one voice: keep the take's own singing there, at a
   matched level.
4. The pieces laid back over the take's band, and the result used as the number's
   `audio\song.wav`.

Then align again with `--retranscribe` (the cached transcript belongs to the old file), build,
and render the singing shots again.
