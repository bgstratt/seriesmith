The script is what the model is told; the references are the pictures it's shown: each
character's sheet, each place's plate. This tutorial works on the Dean episode from
[tutorial 3](/tutorials/screenplay-to-episode) (Dean, Pip, the window seat and the bed).

**You need:** for the wardrobe change and the frame, an **edit model** for pictures
(FLUX.2 Klein edit, Qwen-Image 2.1, FLUX.3 Klein...). Turn and Make 360 need Qwen-Image 2.1
with its multi-angle LoRA, and the tour MiniMax H3: **Settings → Features…** lists each
under *360s and turns*. Krea 2 alone draws from words only.

## 1. Four views, and fixing one

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open a character">
    **Refs** → click **Dean** to open his row. He has four views, three-quarter, side, back
    and face, stitched into one **sheet**: the picture that goes in with every shot he's in.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Find the view that's off">
    Click a candidate to see what made it (its model, settings and prompt); double-click it
    to see it against the live one.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Regenerate only that view">
    Set the view selector to that view (say **back**) and the count beside it to **2**, then
    press **Generate**. Each candidate gets its own seed; the other three views are untouched.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick one">
    Press **Pick** on the candidate you like. The sheet re-stitches by itself, and the shots
    that used the old sheet are marked `stale: ref`, ready for **Re-render stale**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  Have pictures of your own? Drop a file on a slot (or **Upload…**), or drop a whole
  folder on the Refs tab: each file is matched to a slot by its name, and you see the table
  before anything is sent.
</Tip>

## 2. A wardrobe change

The same character in other clothes is a new subject, a **variant** (`of:` in the series
config), with a sheet of its own.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Add the variant">
    Refs → **+ Character**. Name *Dean in teal pajamas*, id `dean_teal`, and set **Variant
    of** (among the card's further fields) to *Dean*. Its design says only what changes:

    ```
    the same boy in soft teal pajamas covered in little yellow stars, barefoot
    ```
  </Step>
  <Step title="Generate its views">
    Generate its four views and pick them. On an edit model each is drawn **from Dean's own
    matching view**, so the face and hair stay his. Select a candidate and read its **drawn**
    row to check: *edited from Dean (three-quarter)*.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Put it in a shot">
    Edit sh040 (pencil, or right-click → **Edit shot…**), change **Who** from Dean to *Dean
    in teal pajamas* (in the script: `who: dean_teal, pip`), and press **Save and generate**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Compare">
    Right-click the new take → **Compare with…** the old one: the same Dean, in teal.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  Make the character's own views first. On a first pass every job is planned before
  anything is picked, so a variant has nothing to edit from and is drawn cold. Pick Dean's
  views, then regenerate the variant.
</Note>

## 3. A tour of the room

A **tour** moves the camera through a place from its plate, and every frame it holds can
become a new plate: another angle of the same room.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the place's Tour">
    Refs → the **window_seat** location → **Tour**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Say the move">
    Pick a move, or write your own:

    ```
    the camera slowly turns from the round window toward the small wooden bed across the room
    ```

    Set how long it runs, and press **Run tour**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Keep a frame">
    H3 renders the move and holds; each held frame lands as one of the place's tour holds
    (**Grab frame** in the viewer keeps another). **Use** copies a hold in as a plate
    candidate, for this location or another (the bed, say: the same room, from where Dean
    sits). **Use and sharpen** also queues a sharpening edit on it.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## 4. Turn and 360

<Steps>
  <Step title="Turn a character">
    On Pip's row, press **↻ Turn** at the top of a view's column (side, back or face). That
    view is drawn by turning the camera around him, from a view he already has (the
    three-quarter, if picked). It's a new candidate, not picked: pick it if you like it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Make 360 of a place">
    On a place's row, under its plate, press **Make 360**. The plate is outpainted into a
    360 panorama.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Look around, and save a view">
    Open the 360 and drag to look in every direction from where the plate stood. Aim at a
    view you want and press **Save and sharpen**: it becomes a new plate of the place.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## 5. A character from a frame

Someone who only appeared in a take (described in `extras:`) can be kept as a character.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Make the shot">
    Add a place, *garden* (Refs → **+ Location**, a description of its own, no **Angle
    of**: it's a place of its own, not an angle of the window seat), generate its plate and pick it. Then add a sequence to the script and render it:

    ```
    # sq03  garden

    ## sh050
    who: pip
    size: wide
    dur: 4
    extras: a big grey mother owl with soft white feathers and amber eyes, waiting on the branch
    Pip lands on a branch of the old oak tree beside his mother, and she wraps a wing around him.
    camera: holds a static shot
    sound: a soft hoot, leaves rustling in the night breeze
    ```
  </Step>
  <Step title="Open the take in the viewer">
    Double-click the take, and play or scrub to the frame where she's clearest.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Right-click the picture itself">
    Right-click **the picture in the viewer** (not the take's thumbnail or the timeline:
    those have no frame to take) → **Character or place from this frame…**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="A new character">
    Choose **A new character**, name her *Mama Owl*, and say who she is in the frame:

    ```
    the big grey owl beside Pip
    ```

    The edit model cuts her out onto a plain background as her three-quarter view; her other
    views are drawn from that one, each to pick as it arrives, and stitched into her sheet.
    She's now a character to cast in any shot.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The same dialog makes **a new place** (the people taken out, the place left as a plate) or
adds the frame as a new candidate **for one there is**. **Use the frame as it is** skips the
edit.
