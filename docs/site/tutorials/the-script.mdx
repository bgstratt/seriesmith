Every shot in seriesmith is a few lines of a markdown script, `epNN.md`, and every
person and place in it is described once in the series config, `series.json`. The build
turns the two into what each video model is told. This tutorial writes a shot by hand,
explains each line, and then edits the same shot in the editor.

The example is *Dean and the Grumpy Star-Horse* (a 2D storybook episode on MiniMax H3).
The [authoring guide](/authoring) is the full format.

## 1. Make the episode's two files

<Steps>
  <Step title="Make an episode to write into">
    In the editor: **Project folders** (the episode dropdown → **Browse and add new…**) →
    the show's folder → **New episode…** (or **New series/project…** for a new show). Or from
    a terminal:

    ```
    seriesmith new Shows\ep01 --title "First Light"
    ```

    Either writes a `series.json` and a script that build, to change into your own.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Open the script">
    In the Shots toolbar, the file-edit icon opens the **Script** window: the episode's
    `epNN.md` as text. The book icon opens `series.json` the same way; the cog opens it as a
    form (**Series settings**). `Ctrl+S` saves either window.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## 2. A shot, line by line

Here is one shot, and the sequence header it sits under:

```
# sq02  purple_beach

## sh040
who: lani, dean
size: cowboy
dur: auto
plate: purple_beach
Queen Lani walks up the beach from frame right and bends to greet Dean at frame left with a kind smile. Dean looks at her in awe.
camera: pushes in at slow speed
LANI: Welcome, Dean. We need a helper today.
sound: soft footsteps on sand
```

Write each line in this order, one per line:

<Steps>
  <Step title="# sq02  purple_beach: the sequence">
    A sequence happens in one place. The word after its id is a location's key in the series
    config, where the place is described and its plate picture named. Every shot under it is
    there unless it says otherwise.
  </Step>
  <Step title="## sh040: the shot">
    Every `##` is a cut: one shot, one clip. Number shots in tens (sh010, sh020) so there's
    room to add sh015 later. The id also sets the shot's random seed, so a re-render of an
    unchanged shot is the same take.
  </Step>
  <Step title="who: the people in it">
    Characters' keys, in order. Each one's picture goes in with the prompt ("Exactly two
    characters appear in this shot..."). Props go on a `with:` line after it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="size: how close">
    From extreme close-up to extreme wide: `ecu`, `cu` (or `close`), `mcu`, `medium`,
    `cowboy`, `mws`, `full`, `wide`, `ews`, or an angle: `pov`, `ots`, `low`, `high`. Cowboy
    is mid-thigh up, and the prompt says so.
  </Step>
  <Step title="dur: how long">
    Seconds (`dur: 4`), or `auto`: as long as the shot's dialogue takes to say. Each model
    renders on its own frame grid, so the length is rounded to it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="plate: the picture behind them">
    Optional. Without it, the shot uses the sequence's location. A closer angle of a place can
    be a location of its own, named here.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The action">
    Any line that isn't a field is the action: what we see, in the present tense, plainly, as
    you'd tell a camera crew. Say where people are in the frame ("from frame right").
  </Step>
  <Step title="camera: one move">
    In plain words, without "The camera": `pushes in at slow speed`, `pans left`, `holds a
    static shot`. It becomes "The camera pushes in at slow speed."
  </Step>
  <Step title="LANI: a line">
    The speaker's key in capitals, as in a screenplay, then the words. How the voice sounds is
    the character's `voice` in the series config.
  </Step>
  <Step title="sound: what we hear">
    The scene's sounds besides the lines: ambience and the sounds of the action.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## 3. The series config behind it

The script names people and places by key; `series.json` says once what they look like,
and the build says it in every shot they're in. That's why Dean looks like himself
throughout.

```json
{
  "style": {
    "look": "a warm 2D storybook cartoon style with soft clean ink outlines, flat muted colors..."
  },
  "subjects": {
    "lani": {
      "kind": "character",
      "name": "Queen Lani",
      "design": "a tall kind alien queen with soft lavender skin...",
      "voice": "a warm, calm, gently musical queen's voice"
    }
  },
  "locations": {
    "purple_beach": {
      "description": "a beach of soft purple sand on an alien planet, two orange suns low in a peach sky..."
    }
  }
}
```

| Field | What it does in the prompt |
|---|---|
| `style.look` | One sentence of how the whole series looks. It opens every shot's prompt |
| a character's `name` | The name the prompt uses (`LANI` in the script is "Queen Lani") |
| a character's `design` | What they look like, said in every shot they're in, beside their picture |
| a character's `voice` | How their lines sound, when voices are generated |
| a location's `description` | The place, and the words its plate is made from |

## 4. A few more lines

```
NARRATOR (V.O.): One sleepy Sunday morning, Dean found a shiny shell.
music: a soft ukulele melody
DEAN (whisper): I can hear the waves!
extras: a few island villagers in woven wraps standing in the drizzle behind him, blurred
```

- **`(V.O.)`**: heard, not seen. The narrator is a character with a `voice` and no picture.
  `(O.S.)` is off-screen but in the scene.
- **`music:`**: the score, under the scene. Leave it out for none.
- **`(whisper)`**: how a line is said. Any short direction works: `(breathless)`, `(flat)`.
- **`extras:`**: people in the background, described so they don't look like your cast.

## 5. Check and build

<Steps>
  <Step title="Save">
    `Ctrl+S` in the Script window. A copy of the old script goes into the episode's
    `_history/`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Build">
    Press **Build** on the episode's card. It compiles every shot for both passes and lists
    any warnings (a line too long for its shot, a name the series config doesn't have). From
    a terminal: `seriesmith check Shows\ep01`, then `seriesmith build Shows\ep01`.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## 6. The same shot, in the editor's form

<Steps>
  <Step title="Edit shot">
    Select sh040 and press the pencil in the Shots toolbar (or right-click → **Edit shot…**).
    The form is the same lines as fields. Beside it is **What the model is told**: the prompt,
    compiled as you type, with the dialogue's pace against the shot's length.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Change the framing">
    Pick **Framing** from its list (medium → cowboy): the prompt's opening changes with it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Change the action">
    Type into **What happens** (*Dean looks at her in awe.*): the words appear in the prompt.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save">
    **Save** writes only this shot's lines into the script, keeping your comments, and
    rebuilds. **Save and generate** also queues a take.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  A line too long for its shot shows in the form's pace line and in the build's warnings.
  Lengthen the shot (`dur:`), set `dur: auto`, or cut words: a line has to fit the seconds
  it's given.
</Tip>
