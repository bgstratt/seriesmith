A model like H3 renders each shot with its own sound: the lines, footsteps, a room tone and often
a bit of music, different in every take. Cut together, that sound changes at every cut. This
tutorial rebuilds the episode's sound on four tracks under the picture, in the order that works:

1. **Dialogue**: only the voices from each take.
2. **Ambience**: one bed of room tone per place, under every shot set there.
3. **Effects**: a shot's own sync sound, where it needs one.
4. **Music**: a score drafted from the script's story beats, cue by cue.

Then the mix, and the export with its stems. Do them in this order: the score is judged against
the sound it sits on.

This continues on the Dean episode from tutorial 13, with every shot rendered.

<Note>
  **Before you start:** open **Features** and install what you'll use under **Sound**: the voice
  stems (Hybrid Demucs), ambience (MMAudio), effects (H3 FL2VA) and score (Stable Audio 3).
  Then **Sound** in the timeline's header unfolds the hits lane and the four tracks under the
  clips.
</Note>

## 1. Voices only

<Steps>
  <Step title="Turn it on">
    Right-click the **Dialogue** lane → **Voices only**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Make voice stems">
    **Make voice stems**, beside it, splits every take the cut plays into its voices and the
    rest: a job per take in Jobs, about a second each once loaded. It counts as it goes (*8/10
    shots split*).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Play all">
    Each take's own music and room tone is gone; only the lines are left. A shot not split yet
    plays its whole sound, with a warning mark on the lane, never silence. A shot with no lines
    in the script is silent on this track.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  A take whose own sound you want to keep (its music, a gasp the model added): its clip's
  **Sound** menu → **Keep its whole sound**.
</Tip>

## 2. A bed for each place

<Steps>
  <Step title="Say what's heard there">
    Refs tab → **Ambience** lists each place, its words, and how many shots it plays under.
    Write a place's words as a sound designer would: the steady bed first, then a few sparse
    events, never music or voices (*a low wind through pines, an owl far off now and then*).
    They're saved as the location's `ambience` in the series config.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Make it">
    Open the place's row → **Make it**. Listen to the takes and **Pick** one.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Hear it under the cut">
    The bed lies on the **Ambience** lane as a dashed block named for its place, looped under
    every shot there and crossfaded into the next place's at the cut. Right-click it for its
    **level**, or **Mute this place**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## 3. A shot's effects

With voices only on, a take's footsteps and doors went too. Put them back where you miss them.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Give the shot a sound: line">
    In the script, or the shot's form: `sound: small boots crunching on snow, a branch creaking`.
    The effects are made from it (else from the action, which can mislead: *about to sing* gets a
    voice).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Make effects">
    Right-click the clip → **Sound → Make effects** (several clips selected: one each). On H3,
    about 10 seconds a shot. The new take is picked into the mix as it lands.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Trim and fade">
    On the **Effects** lane, drag an edge to trim (a voice creeping in at the end), drag the corner
    squares for fades, right-click for its level or another take.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## 4. The score

### Mark the story's beats

The draft reads the script's story structure from comments, which no video model sees, so
nothing renders again:

```
// structure: Pixar Story Spine

// beat: Once upon a time…
# sq01  ...
// beat: Until one day…
# sq02  ...
```

A script written with the script-writing skill has them already. One without: the Draft dialog's
**Structure** list lays the beats out for you and writes them into the script.

### Draft it

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the spotting sheet">
    **Scoring** in the timeline's header → the **Spotting** tab → **Draft…**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Read the draft">
    It shows the cues it would make. Each beat is scored, held low under the lines, a short
    sting, or played dry, by the structure's Music table. Beats in a row with music are one cue,
    so the score changes with the story rather than stopping at every beat. A bedtime story on
    the Story Spine is often one cue across the episode.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write">
    **Write** adds the draft cues (`d01`, `d02`, ...) and their hits. Nothing is rendered yet.
  </Step>
</Steps>

### Render and pick

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open a cue">
    Double-click it on the spotting map. The **Score** tab shows its sections, hits and arc on
    one bar grid.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Adjust">
    The words (**Style**: *solo celesta and soft strings, no percussion*), the tempo, a section's
    energy. Or **Start from a preset** (lullaby waltz, sad and tender, wonder and awe, ...).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Render 8 takes">
    On Stable Audio 3, a few seconds a take on a 5090. Each is measured: how close its loudness
    follows the arc, how near the music lands each hit. The list is ranked, but ranking can't
    hear whether it's good: **listen to the top two or three** and **Pick** one.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Hear it under the picture">
    The pick lands on the **Music** lane, from the cue's in point to its out point, its level
    shaped to the arc. Play all.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  A moment the music must answer (a door slams, the music drops out): double-click the hits lane
  to add a **hit**. A cue's tempo is fitted so its hits fall on bar lines. Render again after
  adding one.
</Tip>

## 5. The mix

- **Levels:** each lane's fader, **M** mute and **S** solo. Music starts at −12 dB, under the lines.
- **Ducking:** music and ambience dip under speech by themselves (music −9 dB, ambience −6 dB).
- **Level the lines** (the Dialogue lane's menu): a quiet take and a loud one brought together,
  at most 9 dB either way.
- **Score forward:** right-click a lane → **Duck region here**, for a stretch where the music
  carries the scene over the lines.

Play all plays the mix as the export will. Every change saves at once; Ctrl+Z undoes it.

## 6. Export

**Export ▾ → Export**. The episode comes out with its mix at the loudness target (−16 LUFS
unless the series config says otherwise), and beside it:

- `<cut>_stems/`: one file per track, `me` (music and effects, for a dub), and `mix`
- a loudness report
- the lines as subtitles, `.srt` and `.vtt`

**Export ▾ → Premiere / Resolve XML** lays the same out on separate tracks, to finish in an editor.
