Directing one shot: how close the camera is, the angle it looks from, and how it moves.
Everything here is picked from pictures and clips, and each pick writes a line of the
script. The example is sh020 of the Dean episode.

## 1. Framing, as pictures

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the shot form">
    Select sh020 and press the pencil in the Shots toolbar (or right-click → **Edit shot…**).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Open Framing, as pictures">
    Under **Framing** and **Length**, open **Framing, as pictures**: the nine sizes as the
    same woman on the same street, extreme close-up to extreme wide, each with how much the
    frame holds (*chest up*, *head to toe*), and the four angles: POV, over the shoulder,
    low, high.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick one">
    Click **Low angle**. **What the model is told**, beside the form, changes with it.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## 2. Camera moves, as clips

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Camera moves, as clips">
    Next to **Camera**, open **Camera moves, as clips**: each move as a short clip of the
    same street, played while you point at it: push in, pull out, pan, tilt, truck, arc,
    POV, handheld...
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick a move">
    Click **Arc around**. It starts the camera line.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set its amplitude, speed and where it goes">
    Choose the amplitude (small to large) and the speed (slow to fast). Under **where it
    goes**, say what the move travels around, toward or reveals: *around Dean*, *toward her
    face*, *revealing the square*. The model frames the end of the move from it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save and generate">
    Press **Save and generate**: the camera line goes into the script (`camera: arcs around
    Dean with small amplitude at slow speed`) and a take is queued.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
  **Say what the camera moves around.** A bare *The camera arcs around.* leaves the model to
  choose, and in the recording it went round the oak tree outside the window: the window
  held still, and the house moved as if on wheels. seriesmith now names the shot's people
  after a bare arc, but it's still best to say it yourself.
</Warning>

## 3. Changing one take's prompt

To try a different camera line on a single take without changing the script:

<Steps>
  <Step title="New take…">
    Right-click the take → **New take like this one…**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Edit the prompt">
    In the dialog's prompt, change the camera sentence (*The camera arcs around Dean.*). Keep
    the seed the same to compare like with like.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Generate">
    Press **Generate proxy take** (or final). Now the camera goes round Dean, and the room
    turns behind him.
  </Step>
</Steps>

If the change is one to keep, put it into the script (the shot form or the Script window):
story edits belong there, not in a one-take prompt.

## 4. Compare the takes

Select sh020's takes and compare them two at a time (**Compare with…**, side by side or
under a wipe), or **Use** each in turn and **Play all**: as written (medium, the camera
still), low angle arcing around the tree, low angle arcing around Dean.
