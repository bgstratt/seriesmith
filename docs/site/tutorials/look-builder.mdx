A series' **look** is one line in its series config (`style.look`): how the whole show is
shot or drawn. It opens every shot's prompt and every picture's. This tutorial changes the
look of the live-action Clips project from tutorials 1 and 2 (Mara at the lighthouse).

## 1. Pick a look from pictures

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Series settings">
    The cog in the Shots toolbar (Advanced mode) → **Series settings** → **Look**. (The
    series wizard's **Series** step has the same row.)
  </Step>
  <Step title="Browse the presets">
    Every look is a picture of the same woman in a red coat at a tram stop, so they compare:
    live action (prestige drama, neon noir, 1970s film, documentary, black and white),
    genres (western, thriller, horror, 80s VHS...), animation (3D animated, 2D cartoon,
    anime, stop-motion) and illustration (watercolor, graphic novel...).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick one">
    Click **1970s film**. It fills the look line, which is still yours to edit.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  A genre's picture shows its world (a frontier town, a desert highway), but the line is
  only the look: light, lens, grade, stock. Your locations and characters decide where the
  story is, so a western look on a city street is a dusty, sun-bleached city.
</Note>

## 2. Build the look from its parts

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Build the look">
    Under the presets, **Build the look** shows a row for each part, a picture for each
    choice: **Medium**, **Camera**, **Lens**, **Film / sensor**, **Light**, **Grade**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick a part at a time">
    Start with the medium, then the camera and lens, the film stock, the light and the grade.
    Each pick adds what a director of photography would say of it (*anamorphic lenses: oval
    bokeh, horizontal lens flares...*), and the line rewrites itself. A second click on a pick
    clears it. A drawn or animated medium folds Camera, Lens and Film away.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Start from a preset, if you like">
    A look preset sets the rows too, so you can pick *1970s film* and change only its grade.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Edit the line by hand (optional)">
    Once you type into the line yourself, the picks leave it alone. **Use the built line**
    puts theirs back.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save as a preset…">
    Press **Save as a preset…** under the line. It's offered under **Yours** in every show on
    this machine. Give its tile a picture with the picture button on it: **Render it** on the
    sample scene, take it **from your work** (a frame of a take, or a picture in Refs), or
    **import a picture**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save the series settings">
    **Save**. The look is written into `series.json`, its picks kept as `style.look_parts`.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## 3. What it does

The look goes into every picture too, and on a model that renders from pictures (H3) the
plate and the character's views carry most of it. So make them again under the new look
before re-rendering the shots.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Regenerate the pictures">
    **Refs** → the place's plate and each character's views → **Generate**, then **Pick** the
    new ones. (Or **Generate missing** after clearing them.)
  </Step>
  <Step title="Re-render stale">
    The shots that used the old pictures are now `stale`. Press **Re-render stale**: each is
    rendered at the same seed, so the new take differs by the look and nothing else.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Compare">
    Right-click the new take → **Compare with…** the old: c001 before, and in the 1970s look.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  A **lens for one shot** is a field of its own (`lens:` in the script, **Lens** in the shot
  form). It's one sentence of the prompt and never changes the pictures, so with a plate's
  sharp focus behind it, it may show little on H3. It shows most on models that draw the place
  from words (LTX-2, Wan).
</Tip>
