seriesmith is driven by two files you write (or have Claude write from a screenplay):

- **`series.json`, the series config:** who and what is in the show (characters, props,
  vehicles), the places, how they look and sound, and how the show is rendered and
  delivered. Written once, shared by every episode.
- **`ep01.md`, the episode script:** every shot in a few lines, in order: where, who, how
  close, how long, what happens, what is said, the camera and the sound.

From those, **Build** makes the shot list; the Refs tab makes the pictures the config
describes (each character's sheet, each prop, a plate for each place); and every shot
renders from its lines and its pictures. Change a line, and only the shots it touched
render again.

This page follows one real scene through: **LAST LIGHT**, a rooftop chase in the rain. The
three files are in the repo as they came out, in
[examples/demo/](https://github.com/bgstratt/seriesmith/tree/main/examples/demo), and the
finished episode is the [trailer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Bc2VXPys6Ak).

## The three files

| File | What it is | Made by |
|---|---|---|
| `screenplay.fountain` | A spec-format screenplay: about a page and a half, three lines of dialogue | by hand |
| `series.json` | The series config: 2 characters, 2 props, a vehicle, 13 places, the look, the render size and the master | Claude, with the script-writing skill |
| `ep01.md` | The episode script: 10 sequences, 27 shots | the same conversion |

To convert your own, see [Screenplay to episode](/tutorials/screenplay-to-episode). The
rest of this page is what the conversion did, and why, so you can write or correct these
files yourself.

## 1. The show, once

Before any shot, the series config settles how the whole show looks and is delivered.
None of it is in the screenplay; all of it has to be right before the first take.

```json series.json
  "series": {
    "id": "last_light",
    "title": "Last Light",
    "fps": 24,
    "width": 1344,
    "height": 768
  },
  "proxy": { "width": 864, "height": 480 },
  "style": {
    "look": "a live-action neo-noir thriller shot at night on 35mm anamorphic lenses: heavy rain catching the light, shallow depth of field with distant neon falling into soft bokeh, hard magenta and cyan neon practicals against deep black shadows, wet reflective surfaces, high contrast and fine film grain"
  },
  "speech": { "pace": "normal" },
```

- **`series`:** the frame rate and the render size (1344×768 is MiniMax H3's own).
- **`proxy`:** the size of the quick draft pass, for judging the cut before the finals.
- **`style.look`:** one sentence that opens every shot's prompt and every picture's: the
  medium, the lens, the light, the grade. It says nothing about the story.
- **`speech.pace`:** how fast lines are spoken, which decides how long a shot with dialogue
  needs to be.

At the end of the file, how dialogue sounds and how the master is made:

```json series.json
  "audio": { "mode": "generate" },
  "upscale": {
    "save_latents": "final",
    "master": {
      "deliver": "1080p", "fit": "crop", "quality": "master",
```

`audio.mode: generate` means the model invents each voice from its description, so nothing
is recorded first. `upscale.master` is the recipe every shot is finished by on its way to
a 1080p master ([Finishing](/tutorials/finishing)).

## 2. Characters: what the camera can see

The screenplay introduces Mara in a line:

```text screenplay.fountain
MARA KESS (20s), a courier in a cropped yellow rain jacket, hood down, braids whipping, sprints between rusted water towers. Strapped across her back: THE CASE, a battered steel briefcase with a seam of blue light pulsing along its edge.
```

and she leaves the script for the series config:

```json series.json
    "mara": {
      "kind": "character",
      "name": "Mara",
      "design": "a lean woman in her twenties with dark brown skin and long black box braids worn loose, a cropped bright-yellow rain jacket with the hood down, a fitted black top, black cargo pants and scuffed black combat boots",
      "sheet": "refs/mara/mara_sheet_4panel.png",
      "voice": "a young woman, low and quick, a little breathless, dry and wry"
    },
```

- **`design`** is said in every shot she's in, beside her picture, so it's what she looks
  like and nothing else. *A courier* is her job, not her appearance: it's gone. What a
  screenplay leaves to the costume department (her skin, her top, her trousers, her boots)
  is spelled out, because the model has no costume department.
- **`sheet`** is her picture: four views stitched into one, made in the Refs tab from the
  design. It goes in with every shot she's in, so she is the same Mara in shot one and
  shot twenty-seven.
- **`voice`** is how her lines sound.

The script then names her by her key, `mara`, and her lines by `MARA`.

## 3. Props and vehicles, and a change of state

THE CASE is a prop with a picture of its own, like a character. When it opens, it's a
different picture, so the open case is a **variant** of it (`of: case`): the same case, its
own sheet.

```json series.json
    "case": {
      "kind": "prop",
      "name": "the case",
      "design": "a battered brushed-steel briefcase with dented corners and a black canvas shoulder strap, a thin seam of pulsing blue light running all the way around the edge of its closed lid",
      "sheet": "refs/props/case.png"
    },
    "case_open": {
      "of": "case",
      "design": "a battered brushed-steel briefcase lying open with its lid raised, grey foam inside cradling a single small seedling sealed in a clear glass bulb, the seedling's leaves glowing a soft blue that lights the foam around it",
      "sheet": "refs/props/case_open.png"
    },
```

The drone is a `vehicle`, which works the same way. In the script, props and vehicles go
on the `with:` line, after the characters on `who:`.

## 4. Places: one angle each

Each slugline becomes a location: a description, and a **plate**, the picture of the place
behind the action. But a plate is one camera position, and a scene shot from several sides
needs one per side. So `EXT. ROOFTOP - WATER TOWERS` became three: the roof, the stairwell
door Voss comes out of, and the aisle Mara runs down.

```json series.json
    "water_towers_door": {
      "of": "water_towers",
      "description": "the same tar rooftop looking toward its stairwell: a rusted steel door in a brick bulkhead under a caged yellow bulb, rusted water towers on steel legs to either side, rain slanting through the bulb's light, magenta neon glow from the left",
      "plate": "refs/_bg/water_towers_door.png"
    },
```

`of: water_towers` marks it as an angle of the same place. The sequence header names the
main location; a shot picks an angle with `plate:`.

## 5. A scene becomes shots

The screenplay's water-towers scene:

```text screenplay.fountain
Her boots smash through a puddle. Neon bleeds across the water.

Behind her, a door BANGS open. VOSS (40s), tall, grey longcoat, mirrored visor over his eyes, steps into the rain. He doesn't run. He doesn't need to.

                    VOSS
          That case isn't yours, Kess.
```

became three shots. Every `##` is a cut:

```text ep01.md
## sh030
who: mara
size: ecu
dur: 1.62
Mara's black combat boot smashes down into a rain puddle on the tar, the magenta neon reflection bursting apart across the water.
camera: holds a static shot
sound: a heavy splash, rain

## sh040
who: voss
plate: water_towers_door
size: full
dur: 3.04
The rusted stairwell door bangs open and Voss steps through it into the rain, then stops on the tar, perfectly still, rain beading on his mirrored visor.
camera: holds a static shot
sound: a steel door slamming against brick, rain hammering, the heavy rustle of a coat

## sh050
who: voss
plate: water_towers_door
size: mcu
dur: auto
Voss walks slowly forward through the rain, unhurried, his mirrored visor turned past camera toward frame left where she runs.
camera: pushes in with small amplitude at slow speed
VOSS (calm, carrying easily over the rain): That case isn't yours, Kess.
sound: rain, slow footsteps on wet tar
```

What changed on the way:

- **One action, one shot.** *Her boots smash through a puddle* is a moment of its own: an
  extreme close-up (`size: ecu`) of a second and a half.
- **The writing became something to film.** *He doesn't run. He doesn't need to.* can't be
  drawn; *stops on the tar, perfectly still* can.
- **Directions on screen.** *toward frame left where she runs* keeps the chase going one
  way across the cut.
- **The line gets its shot and its delivery.** Voss speaks in a medium close-up (`mcu`, so
  his face reads), `dur: auto` makes the shot as long as the line takes, and `(calm,
  carrying easily over the rain)` is how he says it.
- **Every shot has a camera and a sound.** The screenplay leaves both to the crew; the
  model needs them said.

The sequence header above them names the place, and the shots under it are there unless
they pick an angle:

```text ep01.md
# sq02  water_towers
```

## 6. What stays out of the script

```text ep01.md
// Adapted from screenplay.fountain. FADE IN / SMASH CUT TO BLACK are edit decisions: cut them in the timeline.
```

Transitions are made in the edit, not rendered. So are titles and credits (the series' intro
and outro go around the master). A `//` line is a comment.

## Then

<Steps>
  <Step title="Put the two files in an episode folder">
    `series.json` and `ep01.md` together (or import them: **Project folders → Import
    series…**).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Build">
    The shot list for both passes, with any warnings: a line too long for its shot, a name the
    config doesn't have.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Make the pictures">
    Refs → **Generate missing**: Mara's and Voss's sheets, the case, the drone, a plate for
    each place.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Render the shots">
    Shots → **Generate missing**, then **Play all**. Fix what's wrong in the script, and
    **Re-render stale** renders only what changed.
  </Step>
</Steps>
